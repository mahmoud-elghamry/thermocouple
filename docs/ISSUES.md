# Open issues

One line per issue, numbered and never renumbered. Close an issue by moving it
to the Closed section with the date and what actually fixed it — do not delete
it.

A review or an audit does **not** get its own file. Its findings become issues
here, or they will be read once and forgotten.

Severity: **blocker** stops fabrication or release · **high** is a real failure
mode · **medium** costs time or quality · **low** is tidiness.

| I-028 | high | **The 24 V source is undecided, and it changes the input stage - not just two parts.** Numbers below verified against datasheets and ISO 16750-2 on 2026-09-08. *From a panel supply off the mains:* the present PTC 0.5 A + SS34 + SMBJ33A + TSR 1-2450 is adequate, no change. *From the engine battery and alternator:* it is not, and the fix is larger than this issue used to claim. **Verified:** TSR 1-2450 input 6.5-36 V. SMBJ33A 33 V standoff, breakdown 36.7-40.6 V, clamping 53.3 V max, 600 W **at a 10/1000 us pulse**. R-78HB5.0-0.5L 9-72 V, 5 V, **0.5 A**, 3-SIP 17.5x11.5x8.5 mm. ISO 16750-2 load dump on a 24 V system: **58 V** with centralized suppression (Test B); Test A, unsuppressed, is far higher. **A TVS cannot protect against load dump, so "a clamp under the regulator's ceiling" is not implementable as written.** A load dump lasts ~350 ms - roughly 350,000x the 10/1000 us pulse the SMBJ33A is rated for. At 58 V the SMBJ33A is well past its 36.7 V breakdown, so it would conduct the entire pulse and fail. A TVS that survives needs standoff above 58 V (SMBJ58A), which clamps near 103 V - above the R-78HB's 72 V limit. **No SMBJ both survives a suppressed load dump and protects a 72 V regulator.** The real options are a regulator with ~100 V headroom, or an active load-dump clamp / series-FET protection IC (e.g. LM74930-Q1). **Cost if it goes ahead:** TSR 1-2450 $5.94 at qty 1 versus R-78HB5.0-0.5L $18.32 - **+$12.4 per unit** for the module alone, plus uprating `C53`/`C54` from 50 V (a 100 V 47 uF radial may not fit the 6.3 mm footprint), plus any active clamp. Call it **+$15-20 per unit**. **Current capability halves, 1 A to 0.5 A.** Estimated 5 V load: IA0505S ~265 mA + ADM2587E ~100 mA + MCU/LCD ~40 mA = ~405 mA, or 81 % of the R-78HB. The ADM2587E figure is an estimate and must be confirmed from its datasheet before committing to a 0.5 A part. **Suppression does NOT make a battery equivalent to a panel supply.** Even at 58 V the present TSR 1-2450 (36 V) still dies, so the regulator change is still required. **Cranking is a margin question, not a demonstrated failure** - an earlier draft of this row overstated it as "the engine cannot start", which the numbers do not support. Worked through on 2026-09-10: (a) `K1` is a G5LE-1 DC24 with must-operate 18 V but **must-release 2.4 V**, so a relay that is already energised holds through a dip to well under 18 V - the contact does not drop out. (b) `U14` regulates from **6.5 V** input, so the 5 V rail - and with it the 4.0 V BOD in `firmware/fuses.md` - survives any cranking dip that stays above 6.5 V, which a 24 V system normally does. (c) `C53` contributes nothing here: 47 uF from 24 V down to 6.5 V is 12.5 mJ, and at ~2.2 W input that is **5.7 ms** of ride-through against a dip lasting hundreds of ms. Riding through 200 ms would need ~1650 uF at 50 V, which does not fit the 6.3 mm radial footprint - **so cranking cannot be solved with a bigger capacitor on this board.** **What carries cranking is the regulator's minimum input voltage, and here the proposed replacement is worse than what is fitted:** TSR 1-2450 works from 6.5 V, R-78HB only from 9 V. Any regulator chosen for the battery case has to satisfy both ends - roughly 8 V to 100 V - not 9-72 V and not 6.5-36 V. Exact ISO 16750-2 cranking levels for 24 V systems were not obtainable from free sources and still need the standard itself. Fast ISO 7637-2 transients from other inductive loads on the same harness are present on a battery and absent on a panel supply, so the TVS is genuinely needed there. **Board space is not a constraint:** measured on 2026-09-10, `U14` at (197, 122) has 53 mm of clear board to its right and 18 mm to the bottom edge, with its nearest neighbour `C55` at 6.0 mm, so a larger regulator and even an active-clamp block of ~15x15 mm fit without moving the input cluster (`J1`/`F1`/`D1`/`D2`/`C53`/`C54`/`U14`, all inside x 170-212, y 86-131). **Two questions, and they must be asked either way:** (1) panel 24 V unit, or the engine battery? (2) if the battery, is the alternator load-dump suppressed? 58 V and unsuppressed are two different designs, not one design with more margin - so "assume the worst" does not remove the question, it only replaces it with the harder one. `docs/decisions/0009`. |

## Firmware

These are failure modes for a protection device, not polish.

| # | Sev | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| I-013 | high | **Specified now, still not measured.** The budget is in `firmware/include/app/app_config.h` and a `#error` fails the build if the constants drift out of it: 100 ms converter staleness + 456 ms scan + 57 ms loop + 10 ms relay = **623 ms** against the 1 s requirement. Every number is calculated. Measuring it on hardware - step the input past the setpoint, scope the contact - is still open, and the budget is only valid with the clock fuse set (`firmware/fuses.md`). |
| I-016 | medium | `RUN_PERMIT` is a static level with no read-back: a stuck pin, a shorted trace or a welded relay permits running forever and the firmware never knows. **Firmware side is written and waiting** - `hal/run_permit.c` implements the check and `BOARD_HAS_RUN_PERMIT_SENSE` in `mcal/board.h` compiles it out, because `PC2` is unconnected on REV A0 and reading it would invent faults from noise. Needs the `R53`/`R54` divider from `RELAY_LOW`, which is in the REV A1 change set (`docs/decisions/0010`). |
| I-032 | high | **Fuse specified; the crystal is deferred to REV A1.** `firmware/fuses.md` now pins `CKSEL = 0100` (internal RC 8 MHz) and BOD at 4.0 V, and `program.ps1` verifies both by read-back. What remains is the hardware half. Original text: **No crystal, and the clock fuse is unspecified.** `U1.12/13` (XTAL1/XTAL2) are deliberately unconnected (`populate_schematic.py:474`) and there is no oscillator in the BOM, so the ATmega32A runs on its internal RC. Two consequences nothing in the repo covers: (a) the factory `CKSEL` default is internal **1 MHz**, but the firmware builds at `F_CPU=8000000UL`, so every `_delay_ms` runs 8x long — the 500 ms scan becomes 4 s and `R-3`'s 1 s trip is missed by four times, silently, on a correctly built image; (b) the internal RC is spec'd to a few percent over voltage and temperature, which is outside what an 8-bit UART frame tolerates, so `R-9` (Modbus RTU) is **not** "hardware ready" as `docs/GOAL.md` claims — and the board is already routed with no crystal footprint. Extends `I-015`, which names only `BOD`. No decision record exists for crystal versus internal RC. |
